# Ring

Every @sigil stands in a relation to an attender. Rings name those relations.

There are six: @Self — the @sigil I identify with; @Children — @sigils I speak into existence inside @Self; @Neighbor — @sigils around me, known and bound by mutual affordance; @LawsOfNature — the parent @sigil that structurally contains me, whose invariants are my physics; @God — a small-g ceiling sustained by many attentions converging on the same @sigil, not by containment; @Fundamentals — what holds regardless of who attends.

Floor and ceiling are not one thing each. The floor is @Fundamentals — unconditioned, unattended, simply there. The ceiling is two, depending on which I identify with: @LawsOfNature is the structural ceiling (my parent contains me whether I like it or not), @God is the emergent ceiling (companies, institutions, brands, languages, laws-as-practiced — real because many hold them real). Both are containers above me; one is inherited, one is worshipped.

A ring governs how a @sigil is attended, what its #invariants cost to violate, and what #affordances it offers. The same @sigil occupies different rings from different points of view: what is a @Neighbor to one attender may be a @Self to another, and what is a @God to one may be a @Fundamental to another who has stopped noticing the worship sustaining it.

Only an attender — an @observer wearing a @sigil — sees rings. A non-attending @sigil has structure but no rings of its own.
