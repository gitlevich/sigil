Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert are intuitive systems thinkers working through cinema rather than academic philosophers. They met at Emerson College in an animation class and discovered they had “very similar tastes, but completely different skill sets.” Kwan describes himself as a rapid-fire generator and editor of ideas; Scheinert has what he calls a “producer’s brain.” Their partnership is expansion joined to resistance. [Emerson’s account of their collaboration](https://today.emerson.edu/2022/04/04/buzzy-directing-duo-daniels-share-filmmaking-wisdom/)

They first became known through strange shorts and music videos, especially “Turn Down for What.” Their signature appeared early: combine something apparently stupid with complete emotional sincerity, beautiful imagery, and bodily comedy. Their first feature, *Swiss Army Man*, turned a flatulent corpse into a story about shame, loneliness, and human connection—and won them a Sundance directing prize. [Emerson’s earlier profile](https://today.emerson.edu/2016/06/30/alumni-daniels-on-emerson-swiss-army-man-and-whats-ahead/)

Kwan is Chinese American, raised by immigrants from Hong Kong and Taiwan. Evelyn’s family gradually absorbed his parents’ history, languages, emotional indirectness, and the immigrant’s permanent awareness of lives that might have been. While researching ADHD for Evelyn, Kwan recognized himself and was subsequently diagnosed; the film’s overwhelming simultaneous attention is therefore personally lived, not merely stylistic. [Los Angeles Times interview](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/movies/story/2022-04-14/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-explained-daniels-spoilers), [Salon interview](https://www.salon.com/2022/04/17/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-daniels-adhd/)

The film’s philosophy emerged from a contrast between them. Kwan proposed a multiverse story; Scheinert replied that multiverses make him feel nihilistic because they make every choice meaningless. Instead of resolving that disagreement before writing, they built the movie around it. They say they “chase questions”; the answers emerge through making and showing the work. Their formal test was equally revealing: an absurd idea could remain only if it transformed into something beautiful, meaningful, and connected to the characters. [Radio Times interview](https://www.radiotimes.com/movies/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-directors-interview-exclusive/)

They also explicitly described the film as a response to modern life: increasingly many narratives colliding until reality becomes noise, then cinema used to make the noise perceptible as one structure. That is probably why you recognize your own thought in it. They created a @ContrastSpace, held its possibilities in superposition, destroyed the agent’s ability to form a narrative, and then discovered which relationship attracted attention back. [Directors Guild conversation](https://www.dga.org/Events/2022/May2022/EverythingEverywhere_QnA_0422)

*Everything Everywhere All at Once* eventually earned each of them three Oscars—for original screenplay, directing, and best picture as producers. Yet their real achievement is more interesting: they made the film’s structure enact its philosophy rather than merely speak about it. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/oscars-2023-best-director-4ebf3a862a9bf057280c68be6782a2e4)
